# Story and direction

## From brief to direction

1. Write the brief in `templates/brief.md`: audience, length, format, the one feeling the viewer
   leaves with, what must be true (facts, names), what is free to invent, and the backends allowed.
2. Name the **turn**: the single moment the film changes state (noise to silence, fear to
   presence, alone to together). Everything before builds pressure toward it; everything after
   pays it off.
3. Divide the film into 2-4 **worlds**: places or states the viewer can feel the difference
   between (urgency, nature, intimacy). Each world has its own camera, light, palette, sound and
   cutting rhythm. The film moves between them; it does not mix them.
4. Write three loglines in one sentence each, then choose one. A logline names who or what, the
   pressure, the turn and the final image.
5. Write the final image first. It should be able to carry the title.

## Structure

- **Hook within 2 s**: something moves or is heard in the first half second.
- **Build** (about the first quarter to third): pressure, compression, faster cuts.
- **Turn** at 25-35 % for a release film, 45-60 % for a film whose peak is the drop. Silence is
  the strongest turn there is.
- **Payoff**: longer holds, wider frames, the theme stated by picture, not words.
- **Resolve**: the final image, then a beat of silence or a single held note.

The film must read without words. If a beat needs a caption to make sense, redesign the beat.
Captions add a voice, not information.

## Captions and narration

- At most 12 words on screen at once; a short line holds 0.8 s after it settles, a sentence about
  0.3 s per word, plus one second. Fast in, then hold; never fast in, then gone.
- Write captions that sound like a person, not a slogan. Ban stock phrases ("in a world where",
  "journey", "unlock", "streamline").
- Narration, if any, runs about 26-30 words per 10 s. When a take runs long, rewrite the line;
  never speed up the audio.

## Tone

Pick a tone and give it numbers: cuts per minute in each world, the longest hold, the transition
kind. A calm film still needs a contrast to rest against: the stillness only lands if something
was restless first.

## Theme without preaching

Show what is gained, not who is wrong. A film about attention is not against cities or screens:
it shows how much life passes while we are elsewhere, and then lets the viewer arrive.
