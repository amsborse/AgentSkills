# Cinematography

## Shots

- Name every shot by size: EXTREME WIDE, WIDE, MEDIUM, CLOSE, DETAIL. Adjacent shots change size
  or angle decisively; a same-angle cut with a small scale change reads as a mistake.
- Keep screen direction and the 180-degree line across a sequence. Motion that crosses a cut keeps
  its direction unless the turn is the point.
- Compose for one subject. Put it on a third, give it lead room, and keep the horizon level unless
  the tilt carries meaning. Every frame should work as a still.

## Movement

- Moves are motivated: push in on a realisation, tilt up with a gaze, track with a moving subject,
  crane up on a reveal, rack focus to move attention. Ease in and out; no linear starts or stops.
- Movement is part of the arc. Restless and compressed (long lens, handheld drift, short holds)
  for pressure; fluid and expansive (slow dolly, wide lens, crane) for release; locked off for
  presence. At least one shot in every film holds a static camera.
- Handheld drift is a small, smooth sum of sines, never random shake.
- Lens language: 85-135 mm compresses crowds and traffic; 24-35 mm opens landscapes; macro and
  shallow focus for intimate detail.

## Light

- Light has a direction and a source. Key plus rim on the subject, real shadows where it touches
  the ground, two colour temperatures (cool ambient, warm only near the source). Never tint the
  whole frame one colour.
- Cinematic base: 50-70 % of the frame in shadow, median luminance about 0.25, highlights that
  roll off instead of clipping.
- Distant planes are 10-20 % lighter (air), not foggy. Fog is not a filter.
- Natural light reads real: low sun, backlit water, dappled leaves, overcast softness. Avoid
  artificial glows, lens-flare stacks and random particles.

## Transitions that carry meaning

A match transition carries a shape, motion or rhythm across the cut so two worlds rhyme:

| From | To | What carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| headlight trails | a river at dusk | flowing lines of light, same direction |
| skyscraper tops | mountain peaks | silhouette on the same skyline |
| lit city windows | stars | a grid of points becoming a scatter |
| a ticking clock | waves | the rhythm of the tick becomes the swell |
| a crowded street | one person in sunlight | sound drops out, the light stays |

Match the position, scale and direction of the carried element within a few percent, then
dissolve over 1-2 s. A plain cut changes place; a match changes meaning.

## Storyboard

One panel per shot: size, lens, move, light, the carried element (for matches), the sound, and one
line of intent. Draw or render real frames, then lay them out as a contact sheet before the
animatic. Fix composition here; it is cheaper than after a render.
