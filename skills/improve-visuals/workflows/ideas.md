# Lever bank

Levers that lift each rubric dimension. Pick by what the screenshot shows, not by the list
order; one lever per change so the judge can see it. Every lever stays inside the project's
tokens, components and direction.

## 1. Hierarchy and first viewport

- Enlarge the one thing that matters and quiet everything around it (nav weight, secondary
  buttons to ghost style, metadata to a muted tone that still meets contrast).
- Move the answer to the main question above the fold on mobile; cut what pushes it down.
- Group related items so the page reads in 3–5 blocks at a squint.

## 2. Typography

- Raise the ratio between display, heading and body sizes; tighten tracking on large display
  text, loosen it slightly on small caps and labels.
- Cap the measure of body text; balance heading lines (`text-wrap: balance`, `pretty` for body).
- Use tabular numerals for figures that line up; true quotes and dashes.
- One accent weight or italic for emphasis, not three.

## 3. Spacing and composition

- Snap stray values to the spacing scale; make section gaps larger than in-section gaps.
- Align edges to one grid; align text baselines across columns.
- Give the hero and section openers more air than the body; tighten dense lists.
- On mobile, reduce gaps proportionally instead of keeping desktop gaps.

## 4. Color

- Reduce to one accent; turn competing accents into neutrals.
- Tint neutrals slightly toward the accent or the brand so grey stops looking default.
- Use the accent on the single most important action and on state, nowhere decorative.

## 5. Depth and surfaces

- Dark: a soft radial pool of light behind the focal point, a subtle vertical gradient, one
  elevation step between background and cards, a hairline highlight on card tops.
- Light: warm off-white paper, layered soft shadows (small sharp plus large diffuse), cards a
  shade lighter than the page.
- Replace hard borders with a shadow or a tone step where cards blur into the page.

## 6. Imagery and signature visual

- Blend the signature visual into the page with masks and gradient fades; remove its box.
- Crop images to a consistent ratio and focal point; sharpen low-resolution assets.
- Fill dead space with the composition (larger visual, tighter layout), not decoration.
- Diagrams and charts show real data in the page's palette.

## 7. Components and consistency

- One card style across pages: same radius, padding, shadow, title size and action placement.
- Collapse secondary card details into hover, expand or a side panel; keep one primary action.
- Replace one-off buttons and pills with the shared component.

## 8. Interaction states

- Hover: a small lift, tone shift or underline that travels; never a layout shift.
- Focus-visible: a ring in the accent with an offset, visible on both themes.
- Press: a short scale or tone change. Empty and loading: designed states with real copy.

## 9. Motion feel

- Replace snaps with transitions where elements travel to their new place (layout, shared
  element, FLIP).
- One synchronized entrance per section at a moderate pace instead of many staggered pops.
- Numbers count up, charts draw in, progress shows while work runs.
- Reduced motion: keep the state change, drop the travel, still feel designed.

## 10. Detail and finish

- One icon set and stroke width; optical centring of icons in buttons.
- Consistent radii (nested radius = outer radius minus padding).
- Tighten copy: shorter labels, specific headings, no filler.
- Check mobile wrapping, safe-area insets, long words and empty values.
